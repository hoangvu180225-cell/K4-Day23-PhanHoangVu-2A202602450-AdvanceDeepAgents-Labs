# Survey about LLM Agents and Tool Use

## TL;DR
- LLM agents have evolved with various theoretical frameworks, enhancing their capacity for memory and decision-making, enabling them to serve in complex environments [1][2].
- Architecturally, modern LLMs utilize innovative designs, allowing them to interact effectively with multiple tools, thus improving their dynamic functionalities [3][4].
- There is a growing body of empirical studies demonstrating LLM agents' practical applications, including autonomous web actions and sophisticated planning abilities [5][6].
- Current challenges include context retention during multi-tool usage and the static knowledge bases of LLMs, potentially leading to performance issues [7][8].

## Background
Large Language Model (LLM) agents embody advanced AI systems capable of executing tasks autonomously through tool utilization. The significance of LLM agents lies in their adaptability across diverse applications—from customer assistance to complex decision-making in scientific environments. Foundational works and frameworks have emerged, elucidating how these models learn to operate as effective agents by integrating various cognitive architectures and tool categories to improve their efficiency and responsiveness [1][2].

## Theoretical Foundations
The theoretical groundwork for LLM agents encompasses models focusing on memory categorization, social simulation interpretations, and autonomous decision-making frameworks that guide agent behavior in complex scenarios. For instance, recent research discusses novel memory strategies that enhance the memory processing capabilities of LLM agents [1]. Additionally, there is a growing understanding of the interplay between LLMs and social simulation, which accentuates the importance of interpretability in decision-making [2][3].

## Architecture and Design Patterns
LLM architectures and their design patterns have shown a trend towards integrating robust frameworks that facilitate multi-tool orchestration. Noteworthy contributions include frameworks that enhance agent interactions via executable code and orchestrate various AI functionalities in diverse settings [4][5]. The sophisticated architectures have allowed for improved reliability and performance in tasks requiring sequential decision-making capabilities [3][6].

## Empirical Studies and Applications
The landscape of empirical studies validates the effectiveness of LLM agents in real-world tasks. Investigations have revealed LLMs functioning as autonomous agents capable of executing web-based tasks, thereby exploring their interaction with various tools. One pivotal study illustrates the potential of LLM agents in business simulations where they engage in buying and selling practices akin to human strategies, albeit with notable performance gaps [7]. Other studies highlight how user interactions can significantly enhance the efficacy of LLM agents in planning and operational contexts [5].

## Challenges and Future Directions
Despite their capabilities, LLM agents face substantial challenges in tool integration and operational reliability. Research points to issues such as context retention throughout multi-tool interactions and the inherent limitations posed by static knowledge bases, which may contribute to issues like hallucinations [8]. Future research directions highlight the need for improved methodologies that address these challenges, such as dynamic reinforcement learning strategies and enhanced state management approaches to facilitate better tool usage across various contexts [8][9].
